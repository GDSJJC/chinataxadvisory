This blog shares insights drawn from long-term tax practice, focusing on how tax rules operate in real-world situations and how complex issues are addressed through practical application and careful interpretation.

Content is written by Abe Zhao, a tax professional with extensive experience in cross-border taxation, tax dispute resolution, and transfer pricing — more than two decades in international taxation, previously senior partner at a Big Four firm leading cross-border work involving China, plus senior in-house and law-firm roles.

Professional background

Abe began in the United States specializing in international taxation and transfer pricing. Since the early 2000s his work has centered on Chinese tax — inbound investment and outbound expansion by Chinese enterprises.

He spent the majority of his career as an international partner at a Big Four firm, serving as leader of international tax in China and across Asia-Pacific — advising Fortune 500 companies and smaller enterprises on complex transactions, integrating tax with customs, foreign exchange, and overlapping legal frameworks.

As in-house tax director for multinationals (China-focused and global roles), he oversaw expansion into Europe, North America, and Latin America — designing protocols, automation, controversy strategy, and teams; advising on new initiatives; mitigating risk; defending audits.

In law-firm environments he consistently led the most complex projects, where technical accuracy, strategic judgment, and negotiation were equally critical.

Scope of experience

  • Multinationals operating across jurisdictions
  • Senior management navigating regulatory and tax uncertainty
  • Corporate tax functions needing strategy + execution

Industries include manufacturing, technology, consumer goods, hospitality, engineering projects, and digital models. The distinguishing feature: both external advisory and in-house leadership — technically rigorous, operationally grounded.

Nature of work

Focus on complex, non-routine matters where strategy, sequencing, and execution matter as much as interpretation:

  • Tax audits and dispute resolution
  • Transfer pricing planning, documentation, negotiations
  • Cross-border M&A and post-transaction restructuring
  • Supply-chain and operational restructuring
  • Advance pricing arrangements
  • Treaty interpretation and withholding tax

Many matters involve prolonged engagement with authorities, resolved through detailed analysis and sustained negotiation — often without adverse adjustments.

Writing and thought leadership

Active writer and speaker on tax topics; regular professional commentary and technical analysis; invited to academic and professional forums.
