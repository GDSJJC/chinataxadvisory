Exchange of Information Exposes a Hidden Related-Party Relationship Behind a Loss-Making Contract Manufacturer

A Xiamen shoemaker that expanded for years while reporting losses turns out to be related to its sole overseas customer through a shareholder structure built to keep that relationship off the register.

What happened

A Xiamen OEM (leather/PU shoes, ~20 years) reported marginal profits/losses yet grew 4→14 lines, 4M→10M pairs capacity, capital USD 0.4M→9M. Its single US customer dictated production and fixed fees; VAT burden abnormally low; after an earlier adjustment, corporate shareholders became individuals.

Payments ran through an offshore-jurisdiction bank where the manufacturer's shareholder lived. Via STA-approved EOI (9 months), the account proved controlled by the manufacturer's own major shareholder — the same person paying on behalf of the US customer. Nearly 5 years on: +RMB 28.07M taxable income, RMB 2.93M EIT collected.

Framework: Announcement 2016 No. 42 art. 2

Relatedness is the gateway to art. 41 arm's-length adjustment. Art. 2 covers: ≥25% holding (including common holding), financing dependence, IP dependence, purchase/sales/pricing control, overlapping directors/management, family, or substantial common interests — any one suffices.

Testing the facts

The "ordinary trading partner, no cross-investment, no shared management" defense answered only two limbs. Offshore evidence closed the common-interests limb: an unrelated customer settles with the supplier's corporate account, not the shareholder personally offshore. Operational control pointed the same way — a captive processor with no pricing power, fee set to leave perpetual loss.

Implications

Relatedness is substance, not register. Converting to individuals hides the link on paper but not the relationship. For single-customer toll/contract makers, customer direction of volume/spec/fee is itself a signal; expansion-while-losing-money with low tax burden is the classic review profile. Test against all of art. 2, keep arm's-length benchmarking and substance evidence ready before audit/EOI.
