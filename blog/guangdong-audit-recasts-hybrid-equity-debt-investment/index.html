Guangdong Audit Recasts Hybrid Equity as Debt Investment

Back-to-back structure triggers treaty denial and reclassification

A tax audit case in Shenzhen, Guangdong Province, highlights enforcement risks in cross-border share transfers involving Chinese real estate assets.

Transaction Summary

The transaction concerned a Hong Kong–listed real estate investment trust (“REIT”) that acquired 100% equity of a Chinese target company located in Shenzhen’s Futian District. The seller, a Barbados holding company, held the target, which in turn owned multiple commercial real estate properties in the city. The direct share transfer drew scrutiny from local tax authorities, reflecting their focus on potential withholding tax obligations and the valuation of indirect real estate transfers.

In the transaction, the Hong Kong buyer bought 100% equity of the Chinese target from the Barbados seller for approximately USD 760 million. Under the Chinese income tax law, the seller was subject to 10% withholding income tax on gains realized from the equity sale. The gains equal the consideration received by the seller minus the Chinese tax basis in the equity interests sold. Because all Chinese taxes should be settled in RMB, the consideration and the basis must be converted into RMB using the right foreign exchange rates. No treaty relief was available.

The seller incorporated the Chinese target in 2006 with a registered capital of USD 39.5 million (tax basis), when the prevailing foreign exchange rate was 8:1 (1 USD = 8 RMB). At the time of settling the Chinese income tax at the current transfer, the prevailing foreign exchange rate was 6.7:1 (1 USD = 6.7 RMB). While filing the tax return to settle the Chinese tax on the equity transfer, the seller applied the contemporaneous foreign exchange rate of 6.7:1 to convert the sales proceeds into RMB and used the historical foreign exchange rate of 8:1 to convert the tax basis into RMB. The seller argument was that the applicable foreign exchange rate should correspond to the period when the underlying transaction takes place.

The Shenzhen municipal tax authorities disagreed with the approach of using hybrid foreign exchange rates. They took the position that the same foreign exchange rate at the time of equity transfer (i.e., 6.7:1) should be adopted for calculating both the selling price and the tax basis in RMB. The government cited support in STA bulletin 2017-37. This approach caused a reduction of the PRC tax basis in RMB and an increase in the amount of Chinese income tax from RMB 478 million to RMB 483 million. As a result, the tax authorities imposed a RMB 5 million tax adjustment to the seller (about USD 700K).

Practical Lessons

Normally the tax authorities focus on valuation issues in a direct share transfer investigation. In the current case, the buyer and the seller were not related and the tax authorities did not find a good reason to challenge the amount of consideration from a transfer pricing standpoint. However, since the Chinese RMB has appreciated against the USD significantly in the last 15 years, the selection of foreign exchange rate may have a material impact on the tax computation.

Prior to closing, it recommended that the seller meet with the in-charge tax bureau and get aligned with the tax officials in advance on the foreign exchange rate to be used in the withholding income tax return to avoid potential dispute down the road. Because the seller is non-Chinese and needs to remit foreign currency into China to settle the Chinese income tax owed, agreeing on the foreign exchange rate beforehand will also help ensure that the seller wires in a sufficient amount of foreign currency to satisfy the RMB denominated Chinese tax obligation. Meanwhile the buyer should also ensure, through protective clauses in the SPA, that the right amount of PRC income tax is settled by the seller on time, because the buyer arguably may have a withholding obligation (despite that it is non-Chinese) on such tax and could be on the hook if the seller ultimately underpays Chinese tax on the gains.
