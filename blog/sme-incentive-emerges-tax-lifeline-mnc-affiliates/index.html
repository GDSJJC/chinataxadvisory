SME Incentive Emerges as Tax Lifeline for MNC Affiliates

Expanded STI rules let smaller foreign subsidiaries tap ultra-low rates

An income tax preferential program lesser-known to MNCs operating in China is the “SME Taxpayer Incentive” or the STI. Compared with other preferential tax regimes in China, the STI imposes fewer qualification requirements in general. For instance, unlike the high-tech incentive, the STI makes no demand on IP ownership or the level of entrepreneurial R&D activities. Other than the upper limits on the operating scale, any business that is not engaged in a restrictive or prohibitive sector may enjoy the tax benefits.

Why It Matters for MNCs

The Chinese government granted tax relief to SMEs because they create the largest number of jobs in the domestic economy. However, the STI was often considered less relevant for MNCs, which tend to conduct more significant operations in China than allowed by the STI. This situation has changed to some degree since 2019, when the Chinese central tax authorities and the Ministry of Finance jointly issued Bulletin 13 and considerably broadened the scope of SME for the purposes of STI.

Under the new Bulletin 13, a Chinese taxpayer can be an SME for enterprise income tax purposes if it meets the following three criteria simultaneously:

  • The taxable income of the entity during the year is no more than RMB 3 million (or about USD 400K).

  • The total asset of the entity during the year is no more than RMB 50 million (or about USD 7 million).

  • The total headcount of the entity, includes both its own employees and seconded personnel, does not exceed 300 during the year.

  • The total asset and the total headcount should be computed as quarterly averages during the year. The quarterly figures are calculated as the simple average of the beginning-of-the-quarter and the end-of-the-quarter balances.

A qualified SME can enjoy a reduced enterprise income tax rate of 5% for taxable income not exceeding RMB 1 million (USD 140K); for the portion of taxable income exceeding RMB 1 million and below RMB 3 million (USD 400K) the applicable enterprise income tax rate is 10%. If the annual taxable income of an entity exceeds RMB 3 million, it fails to be SME and the regular enterprise income tax rate of 25% applies to the entire amount of taxable income.

Practical Takeaways

The expanded scope of SME described above means that while the larger taxpayers remain off limit, more MNC’s subsidiaries in China may qualify in 2020 given the potentially negative impact on business performance of the ongoing Covid-19. Some of my MNC clients have recently obtained STI for their selected affiliates in the PRC. Companies are reminded to assess the qualification standards and where appropriate, proactively plan themselves into the scope of the program.

The STI incentive mentioned above is mainly with respect to Chinese enterprise income tax. There are similar but not identical concepts in other categories such as VAT, but social security contributions, and stamp duty, etc., that provide preferential treatment to enterprises whose operations are within certain threshold scales. Different qualification criteria apply for accessing benefits in these tax categories other than enterprise income tax.
