Shanghai Court Clarifies VAT Burden in Contract Dispute

Ambiguous “VAT-exclusive” clause leaves seller liable for tax

A recent commercial dispute has underscored the importance of clearly defining value-added tax (“VAT”) responsibilities in contracts. A 2023 court ruling in Guizhou Province highlights the risks associated with ambiguous VAT provisions, particularly in cases involving “VAT-exclusive price” clauses. This case serves as a critical reminder for businesses to address these risks to minimize tax-related issues and avoid potential disputes.

Case Overview

In 2015, Company A (“seller”), a supplier of commercial concrete, signed a sales contract with Company B (“buyer”), which needed concrete for a construction project. The agreement, a standard form contract drafted by the seller, specified the unit price of the concrete and noted that the price was VAT-exclusive.

From October 2015 to May 2020, the seller delivered concrete under the contract, declared and paid VAT to the tax authorities based on the VAT-exclusive price, and issued VAT invoices to the buyer. The seller argued that, since the contract explicitly stated a VAT-exclusive price and the buyer could claim input VAT credits for the VAT paid by the seller, the buyer should gross up its payment to cover the VAT. This would allow the seller to receive the full VAT-exclusive amount specified in the contract after accounting for its VAT obligations.

This interpretation led to a disagreement, prompting the seller to file a lawsuit against the buyer.

Court Ruling

The central issue was whether the buyer should bear the VAT’s economic burden when the contract did not explicitly provide for it.

The court observed that, although the contract specified VAT-exclusive pricing, this alone was insufficient to clarify which party should bear the VAT’s economic burden. Referring to the principle of “whoever claims must provide evidence” under the Civil Code of the People’s Republic of China, the court ruled that the seller, as the legal VAT taxpayer, bore the burden of proof.

In the absence of explicit language in the contract stipulating that the buyer was responsible for the VAT, the court dismissed the seller’s claim.

Key Takeaways

This case emphasizes the importance of drafting precise and unambiguous tax provisions in contracts. While the seller assumed that stating a VAT-exclusive price was sufficient to shift the VAT burden to the buyer, the court did not support this position due to the lack of explicit contractual terms. The seller’s assumption may have seemed commercially logical, given that the buyer could use the seller-paid VAT as input credit, but without clear stipulations, the court ruled against it.

The use of a standard form contract further complicated the matter. Under Article 498 of the Civil Code of the People’s Republic of China, any ambiguity in a standard form clause must be interpreted against the party that provided the contract. This safeguard is designed to prevent contract drafters from unfairly leveraging their position. In this case, the lack of clarity in the tax clause left the seller vulnerable to an unfavorable ruling and created potential exposure in other transactions using the same contract template.

In a nutshell, while this may not matter as much in intercompany agreements, contracts between third parties should not only specify whether prices are VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive but also clearly define which party bears the economic burden of VAT. Companies using standard form agreements should ensure their tax and legal teams review contracts meticulously to eliminate ambiguities. Clear and precise tax provisions are essential to avoid conflicting interpretations and reduce the risk of commercial disputes.
