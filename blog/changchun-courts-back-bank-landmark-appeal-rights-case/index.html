Changchun Courts Back Bank in Landmark Appeal Rights Case

Ruling confirms 60-day appeal clock begins on actual tax payment date

A recent tax litigation in Changchun, Jilin Province, where the local tax authorities lost in both the district and intermediate courts, sheds light on a key judicial interpretation concerning taxpayers’ eligibility for China’s administrative appeal process.

Overview of the Dispute

In Chinese tax controversies, when taxpayers receive unreasonable adjustment decisions from the field auditors, administrative appeal is a critical mechanism to seek rectification. When a taxpayer disagrees with the assessment in a final decision letter from the tax auditors, existing Chinese tax laws and regulations dictate that the taxpayer must settle the disputed tax (or place a satisfactory tax surety bond) before it can file an administrative appeal to the upper-level reviewing authorities. In addition, should the taxpayer wish to file an administrative appeal, it must observe a “60-day window.”

The taxpayer, a local bank, was embroiled in a tax audit led by the 3rd Tax Investigation Bureau of the Changchun State Tax authorities for its 2009-2016 tax years. On July 4, 2018, the taxpayer received a first notice from the auditors, indicating that the taxpayer owed about USD 25 million of tax and should pay up within 15 days of receiving the notice (i.e., due date is July 19, 2018). After the taxpayer objected, the tax authorities issued a second notice, requesting settlement by August 3, 2018.

The taxpayer refused to pay and received a 3rd notice on August 8, 2018, demanding that it should either settle by August 16, 2018 or face enforcement measures. This time, the taxpayer yielded and paid the imposed tax and interest on August 15, 2018. On October 8, 2018, the taxpayer commenced administrative appeal with the Changchun State Tax authorities, formally expressing disagreement with the tax adjustment from the field auditors. The Changchun State Tax authorities rejected the administrative appeal application, on the ground that the 60-day window began on the settlement deadline stated in the first notice letter (i.e., July 19, 2018) and had expired by the time the appeal was submitted on October 8, 2018.

The taxpayer filed a lawsuit at the District Court over the Changchun tax authorities’ refusal to accept the administrative appeal application. After reviewing the facts and legal support, the District Court agreed with the taxpayer and ruled that the 60-day window should start from the date of the actual settlement (i.e., August 15, 2018), and the administrative appeal date (i.e., Oct. 8, 2018) was within the 60-day time frame.

The District Court further concluded that observing the payment deadline in the first notice is not a precondition to access administrative appeal and hence overruled the Changchun State Tax Bureau’s decision to reject the taxpayer application. The Changchun State Tax Bureau appealed the case with the Changchun Intermediate Court, but was defeated again in 2019 and was ordered to redress.

Practical Takeaways for Taxpayers

The case is a significant victory for taxpayers. Historically during tax audits, some local tax authorities often hastily issued tax judgment letters with a 15-day settlement deadline without giving adequate considerations to taxpayers’ arguments, in order to exert maximum pressure. Taxpayers frequently felt compelled to pay by the deadlines in these letters, even though their cases were strong and the tax assessments were unjustified. Their concern was that the deadline from the field auditors automatically would trigger the 60-day window, and missing the deadline would jeopardize the availability of an administrative appeal.

The Changchun case provides a valuable precedent for taxpayers struggling with decision in similar situations and could be used as support during negotiations and communications with the tax auditors. The case once again shows that, contrary to myths, proactively defending against aggressive positions from tax auditors that lack legal basis can yield great results. While not all cases are the same, they should all be examined and analyzed not only on the technical side but also from the procedural standpoint so that the right defense and communication strategy can be developed to protect taxpayers’ rights.
