Shanghai Court Rejects VAM-Linked Tax Refund Claim

Performance compensation ruled separate from original share transfer

On August 16, 2024, the Shanghai Third Intermediate People’s Court disclosed a case in QingPu District, Shanghai, involving a triggered Valuation Adjustment Mechanism (“VAM”) and a tax refund application. The case centered around a share transfer in which the target company failed to meet its performance goals, triggering compensation under a VAM agreement. As a result, the transferor requested a refund of the previously overpaid income taxes. The tax authorities, however, denied the refund request. The taxpayer filed two administrative lawsuits in Shanghai against the tax authorities but lost both.

Case Summary

In 2016, the taxpayer (“seller”) signed an “Asset Purchase Agreement” and a VAM agreement with a buyer for the sale of 50% of the shares in a target company (“share transfer”). The seller guaranteed that the target company would achieve a net profit goal each year from 2016 to 2019. If the target company failed to meet the guaranteed net profit, the seller would be obligated to pay performance compensation as stipulated in the VAM agreement. In 2017, based on a transaction price of USD 82 million and a tax basis of USD 0.7 million, the seller paid an income tax of USD 16 million at a rate of 20% on the capital gains.

Later, the target company failed to meet the net profit goals for 2018 and 2019, and the seller made performance compensation to the buyer for these two years. The seller claimed that the performance compensation led to an overpayment of USD 7.7 million in income tax declared in 2017 for the share transfer and applied for a refund from the QingPu Tax Bureau. On November 8, 2022, the QingPu Tax Bureau denied the refund request. The seller then sought administrative review from the Shanghai Municipal Tax Bureau but also got rejected. The seller then filed two lawsuits in Shanghai against the tax authorities, first with the Shanghai Railway Transport Court, and then the Shanghai Third Intermediate People’s Court. Both courts denied the taxpayer’s tax refund claim, unfortunately.

Core Issue

The key issue in this case is whether the performance compensation should be characterized as a separate transaction or an integral part of the original share transfer in 2016.

The taxpayer argued that the tax paid on the 2016 share transfer was provisional, as the consideration of the transaction was subject to potential future adjustments depending on whether the target company could meet its 2018 and 2019 performance goals. The taxpayer stated that the tax authority should respect the VAM arrangement of the transaction under the Chinese civil law. Specifically, the taxpayer believed that the triggered VAM led to a return of compensation to the buyer, which should have been deducted from the previously declared share transfer proceeds, and the corresponding overpaid income tax should be refunded.

The courts held that, after receiving the transfer proceeds under the original share transfer agreement, the taxpayer settled income tax based on the proceeds, and the tax paid then was final rather than provisional. Once the share transfer consideration was determined and the income tax was settled, any subsequent payment between the parties represented a new taxable event and required separate tax treatment. The taxpayer’s receipt of the purchase price and payment of related income tax occurred in 2016 and 2017 respectively. The subsequent performance compensation passed to the buyer due to the target company’s underperformance in 2018 and 2019 did not alter the original transaction’s tax treatment. Therefore, the courts rejected the taxpayer’s argument that the share transfer and the performance compensation should be viewed as one taxable event.

Comments

In share transfers, the case shows that the tax treatment of performance compensation under VAM agreements in China remains highly uncertain. Some local tax authorities, such as the QingPu tax bureau in Shanghai, consider the share transfer and performance compensation to be separate transactions, meaning that compensation due to unmet performance targets does not result in adjustments to the original share transfer value. In contrast, other local tax authorities view the share transfer and VAM agreements as a single transaction, with performance compensation treated as an adjustment to the original transaction’s price, allowing for a tax refund if the net share transfer consideration is reduced as a result of VAM. There have been successful cases where tax refunds were granted under such circumstances.

In practice, given the varying approaches of local tax authorities, taxpayers should communicate proactively with the in-charge tax offices before the transactions to understand their stance on the tax characterization of VAM agreements. Additionally, both parties should assess the potential tax impacts and uncertainties arising from performance compensation in advance, addressing risks in the agreement. When drafting the transaction agreement, the parties should also consider various legal provisions to strengthen the connection between the share transfer and performance compensation. These measures can reduce the likelihood of future tax surprises.
