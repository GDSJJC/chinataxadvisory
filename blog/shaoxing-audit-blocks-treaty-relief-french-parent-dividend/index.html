Shaoxing Audit Blocks Treaty Relief for French Parent Dividend

Authorities invoke 12-month holding rule to deny 5% WHT rate

A recent China tax audit case in Shaoxing, Zhejiang Province, highlights the challenges of applying income tax treaty benefits to outbound dividend payments.

Background of the Case

In the case, a WFOE distributed dividend to its French parent in March 2019, out of its undistributed earnings from the 2017 fiscal year. Under China’s domestic tax law, the dividend was subject to 10% withholding. The France-China income tax treaty prescribes a preferential 5% dividend withholding tax rate to a qualified 25% shareholder. The French parent took a treaty position on the withholding rate. The WFOE, being the withholding agent, submitted a treaty recordal to its in-charge tax bureau in the city of Shaoxing, and withheld 5% tax on the dividend.

The in-charge tax bureau examined the case subsequently. Recent tax audit cases on dividend withholding tend to revolve around whether the foreign shareholder possesses enough physical substance in its residence jurisdiction to be a beneficial owner. If the answer were no, the treaty withholding rate would be denied even if the foreign shareholder can present a valid tax residency certificate and meet other requirements. In the current case, the French parent was engaged in the active business of manufacturing plastics and minerals in France and incurred substantial personnel and operating expenses. The tax authorities acknowledged that the French parent was a beneficial owner of the Chinese dividend.

Audit Findings and Adjustment

However, through cross-data referencing with other government agencies, the tax officials discovered that the French parent acquired the shares of the WFOE from a group affiliate in Luxembourg one month before the dividend was declared and distributed. Under the STA bulletin 2009-81, the Chinese tax authorities imposed a unilateral “12-month shareholding period” requirement on all foreign companies seeking the 5% treaty withholding rate on dividend. As the French parent owned the shares of the WFOE for only one month before the distribution, the Chinese tax authorities challenged the treaty position.

Another Chinese tax regulation, STA Bulletin 2010-75, states that if a Chinese company distributes dividend from earnings associated with fiscal periods at least 12 months before the distribution date, even a less-than-25% foreign shareholder at the time of distribution may potentially access treaty dividend withholding rate. In the Shaoxing case, the WFOE extrapolated from this provision and argued that since the 25% shareholding requirement was relaxed for dividend from earnings more than 12 months ago (i.e., the earnings were from the 2017 fiscal year), so should the “12-month shareholding period” requirement be loosened up. The tax authorities disagreed, and imposed an audit adjustment on the WFOE for the under-withheld tax.

Key Takeaways for Treaty Planning

This case underscores the key requirements that must be met to qualify for the reduced 5% dividend withholding tax rate under most of China’s income tax treaties. While not all treaties offer the 5% rate, particularly some of the older ones, where it is available, foreign shareholders typically must satisfy all of the following conditions:

  • Be a tax resident of a treaty-partner jurisdiction;

  • Be the beneficial owner of the dividend income from China;

  • Maintain the required 12-month continuous shareholding period prior to the dividend distribution; and,

  • Hold at least a 25% ownership stake in the Chinese company during that minimum holding period.

When facts are nuanced or the holding structure is complex, a technical analysis is essential to evaluate whether all four conditions are met. This is particularly important in the current enforcement environment, where Chinese tax authorities increasingly decline to offer advance rulings and instead place the burden of risk on the withholding agent. In this Shaoxing case, for example, had the dividend been distributed in February 2020 or later, the foreign shareholder would have met the holding period threshold, making the 5% treaty rate applicable. The timing of the distribution thus played a pivotal role in determining treaty eligibility.
