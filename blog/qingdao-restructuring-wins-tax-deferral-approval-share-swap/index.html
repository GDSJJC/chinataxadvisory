Qingdao Restructuring Wins Tax Deferral Approval in Share Swap

U.S. parent’s transfer to UK subsidiary clears six hurdles for special tax treatment

A recently reported internal restructuring case in Qingdao, Shandong Province offers insights into how Chinese tax authorities may approach intra-group share transfers, particularly those executed without cash consideration.

Transaction Overview

In this case, the U.S. parent company (“USP”), headquarters of a U.S.-based multinational group, held a 75% ownership interest in a Chinese operating subsidiary (“China Co”) based in Qingdao. As part of an internal reorganization aimed at streamlining the group’s global equity structure, USP transferred its 75% equity stake in China Co to its wholly owned subsidiary in the United Kingdom (“UK Co”) in December 2016.

The transfer was executed on a share-for-share basis, with UK Co issuing new shares to USP in exchange for the interest in China Co. No cash consideration was involved in the transaction.

Conditions and Outcome

Under the Chinese domestic rules, the equity restructuring above resulted in a change of ownership in China Co. Any built-in gains in the 75% equity interest of China Co being transferred in the restructuring would normally be subject to a 10% Chinese income withholding tax. Where the restructuring qualifies for “special tax treatment” under the PRC internal tax reorganization rules, the Chinese withholding tax could be deferred through PRC tax basis preservation.

In order to enjoy the income tax deferral treatment for this particular transaction, all of the following conditions should be met:

  • The transaction has a reasonable business purpose and does not aim for tax exemption, reduction or deferral as one of its principal objectives;

  • At least 85 percent of the consideration should be “qualified” shares;

  • A minimum of 50% of China Co’s equity interest must be transferred;

  • USP should commit to keeping the shares of UK Co for at least 36 months after closing (”continuity of interest”);

  • The substantive business operations of China Co should remain unchanged for at least 12 months following the consummation (”continuity of business enterprise”); and

  • And future taxable disposal by UK Co should not lead to a reduction of Chinese withholding tax rate under the China-UK treaty compared than if USP sold China Co under the China-US treaty.

USP took a tax position that all six conditions were satisfied. Through a Chinese tax agent, USP filed a tax recordal with the local Chinese tax authorities in charge of China Co in April 2017 and argued its case for qualification. It explained in detail why each of the conditions above was met and submitted a written statement promising that the “continuity of interest” and “continuity of business enterprise” requirements would be fulfilled. After review and negotiation, the tax authorities endorsed the “tax deferral” position taken by USP.

Practical Insights

In practice, the usual difficulty in securing the “special tax treatment” is with condition 1 (”business purpose”), as it is more subjective than the other five. In this case, USP presented the business reasons for the restructuring persuasively. With strong factual support and analysis, it convinced the tax officials that the transaction was not motivated for tax reasons, and the commercial benefits outweighed any tax advantage incidental to the transaction. The outcome is encouraging for other similar restructuring cases.

The tax recordal for this type of restructuring needs to be submitted to the tax officials within 30-days after closing, with a package of information and arguments included. Otherwise, the “special tax treatment” could be denied on procedural ground. Advance dialogues with the local tax authorities are recommended to understand their concerns so that these concerns can be specifically addressed in the information package and explained with emphasis in subsequent meetings.

On a side note, in this case, because the UK-China treaty offers a lower dividend withholding rate than the US-China treaty, for undistributed earnings of China Co before the closing, any associated dividend to UK Co after closing cannot qualify for the lower dividend withholding rate under the China-UK treaty, once a “tax deferral” position is taken on the equity transfer.
